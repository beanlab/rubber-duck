## Working definition

**Agent Evaluation is the systematic collection of reproducible evidence about whether an AI system—model, prompts, tools, orchestration, state, environment, and human interaction together—satisfies specified constraints and how well it achieves its intended purpose over representative and adversarial situations.** It combines deterministic checks of properties that have executable or formal oracles with empirical estimates for stochastic, semantic, human, and contextual qualities; reports uncertainty, variation, costs, and failure slices; and continues after deployment because the system and its operating distribution change. This definition synthesizes the system-level view in [Anthropic’s agent-evaluation guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents), multi-metric evaluation in [HELM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09110), and NIST’s lifecycle-oriented [AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) and [TEVV terminology draft](https://www.nist.gov/document/outline-proposed-zero-draft-standard-ai-testing-evaluation-verification-and-validation).

## Short glossary

- **Agent evaluation:** Evaluation of the deployed system boundary—model plus prompts, memory, tools, controller, environment, and interfaces—not merely the base model.
- **LLM evaluation:** Measurement of a language model or model-based system on defined tasks and qualities; the unit may be a model response rather than an autonomous trajectory.
- **Prompt evaluation:** Controlled behavioral comparison of systems that differ in prompt content/version. It is not literary review of prompt text.
- **Evaluation:** The broad quantitative and/or qualitative determination of whether, or to what degree, a target meets criteria.
- **Testing:** Executing a specified procedure on selected cases to obtain evidence. A passing finite test suite is not a proof over all inputs.
- **Benchmarking:** Standardized comparative testing on a shared suite. It supports comparison but does not by itself validate fitness for a local intended use.
- **Verification:** Evidence that specified requirements are met (“built to specification”). A deterministic assertion verifies only the observed case; **formal verification** can prove a property of a formal model under explicit assumptions.
- **Validation:** Evidence that the system meets its intended use and user/context needs (“the right system”), which usually requires domain and real-world evidence.
- **Model checking:** Exhaustive algorithmic checking, usually over a finite/abstract state model, against a formal property; counterexamples expose violating paths.
- **Runtime verification:** Checking an actual execution trace against a formal specification during or after execution; it offers strong trace-level conclusions, not coverage of unobserved executions.
- **Property-based testing:** Generating many inputs or action sequences to seek counterexamples to invariants, often shrinking failures. Normally empirical testing, not mathematical proof.
- **Simulation / simulated user:** An executable model of a user or environment used to elicit interactive behavior. Results are valid only to the extent the simulator represents the target population and behavior.
- **Red teaming:** Goal-directed adversarial search for harmful behavior, policy bypasses, or security failures. It emphasizes discovery and worst cases rather than average quality.
- **LLM-as-a-judge:** A model used as a semantic grader. It is a noisy measurement instrument that must be versioned and validated against independent human/executable evidence.
- **Neuro-symbolic evaluation:** A hybrid in which neural models interpret or generate candidates while symbolic rules, solvers, types, databases, or theorem provers check formalizable properties.
- **Trace / trajectory:** Ordered events from a trial: messages, model/tool calls, arguments, observations, state changes, retries, errors, timing, and final artifacts.
- **Trial / attempt:** One stochastic execution of one scenario. Repetitions estimate within-scenario reliability.
- **Slice:** A meaningful subset (agent type, difficulty, user persona, language, tool, risk category, conversation length, etc.) analyzed separately to prevent aggregate scores from hiding failures.
